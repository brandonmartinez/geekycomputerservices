Ludington, Michigan · 2003–2005

Geeky Computer Services was a real, licensed, DBA-registered computer business. It fixed PCs, built web sites, and wired up small offices in Mason County. It was founded on September 30, 2003 by a fifteen-year-old, and it shipped twelve releases of its own web application in ten months.

The domain lapsed in March 2005. This page is the business rebuilt from its own source code, twenty years later.

Home page, May 2004

Leave It to the Geeks

Welcome to Geeky Computer Services. We are a newly founded business out of Ludington, Michigan. Specializing in computers and technology, we offer many different services to businesses, non-profit organizations, and of course, the home users. Some of these services include web site design and setup, PC repair, PowerPoint slide show creation, document conversions, and more.

Why we started

Geeky Computer Services is a newly founded business out of Ludington, Michigan. We started September 30, 2003. Since then we have been working to help small businesses, non-profit organizations, and the home user benefit more from computers and technology.

We formed our business with a few goals in mind. Firstly, we wanted to get a good start and being to know more people that we can help. Secondly, make it a customer driven business, allowing all the new people we have met to truly customize the services that they need.

Also, we believe if you want the job done right, then find the right people to do it. That is why we started this business. If you need PC help, a web site created, networking computers, or anything else that is computer related, should you not find the people that specialize in computers? The ones that make computers their lives and more? GCS is made up of those people, the computer geeks. Like our motto says, “Leave It to the Geeks!”

The fine print

This is the part nobody expects. The service contract was written out in full, published on the site, and explained — including why the business could only legally operate in one county.

x. Payment of fees. In order for Geeky Computer Services to remain in business, payments must be made promptly. Delinquent bills will be assessed a $15 charge if payment is not received within 10 days of the due date. If an amount remains delinquent 30 days after its due date, an additional 5% penalty will be added for each month of delinquency. … Regardless of the place of signing of this agreement, the client agrees that for purposes of venue, this contract was entered into in Mason County, Michigan, and any dispute will be litigated or arbitrated in Mason County, Michigan. Please pay on time.

The reason for this to be in the contract is that our business is under DBA licensing. Because of this limiting business license, all conduction of it must be done in our county. This is just to protect us from any legal actions that may be taken and to prevent a contract from becoming void (meaning we lose payment and you on service). We have looked this over many times, and until GCS becomes a corporation, this is the method we must use. It should not be any inconvenience to you, but if it is we are sorry for it.

The story of this page

Geeky Computer Services was hosted at DiscountASP.NET under the account name geekycomput. The hosting was cancelled on March 13, 2005, and the domain registration expired four days later on March 17. From founding to shutdown the business ran about seventeen months, roughly ten of them with a live web site.

Everything you have just read survived because every release was zipped and kept: twelve archives, from Version 1_0_0 through Version 3_0_2, carried forward across two decades of machines. The words are all from those files or from the single Internet Archive capture. Nothing here was rewritten, modernized, or invented — the typos, the “instant messagin friends,” and the plural of virus are all original.

Some things have been deliberately left out. The customer database, the sample invoices, the client names and phone numbers, and the home address and phone number that were printed on the 2004 contact page are not published here. The rest is exactly as it was.

The person who wrote all of this was fifteen years old, and went on to spend his career building software. This was the first version of that.
